# Proposal Summary

## Aim

The primary aim is to examine the relationship between error-related frontal theta synchrony and post-error slowing (PES).

The secondary aim is to compare the split-half reliability of error-trial theta synchrony and ΔERN at matched numbers of usable error trials.

## Objectives

1. Compare error- and correct-response ERP amplitudes at FCz.
2. Compare frontal theta synchrony between error and correct trials.
3. Examine the between-participant association between error-trial synchrony and PES.
4. Compare the Spearman–Brown-corrected split-half reliability of theta synchrony and ΔERN using matched usable error-trial counts.
5. Assess the stability of the reliability comparison across repeated random trial partitions.

## Hypotheses

### H1 — Error-related negativity

Error responses will produce a more negative mean response-locked ERP amplitude at FCz than correct responses during 0–100 ms after the response.

Prediction: ΔERN = ERN − CRN < 0.

### H2 — Frontal theta synchrony

Frontal theta synchrony, measured using dWPLI between FCz and lateral-frontal electrodes, will be greater on error trials than correct trials.

### H3 — Synchrony and post-error slowing

Individuals with stronger error-related theta synchrony will show greater PES.

PES = RT(E+1) − RT(E−1), using valid correct responses immediately surrounding an error.

### H4a — Reliability comparison

Error-trial theta dWPLI will show higher Spearman–Brown-corrected split-half reliability than ΔERN at matched numbers of usable error trials.

### H4b — Robustness analysis

Examine the stability of the H4a reliability comparison across repeated random splits of the same trials.

This is a robustness analysis, not a separate formal hypothesis.
